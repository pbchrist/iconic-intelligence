02 / HOW IT WORKS

Four steps.
Nothing hidden.

The same test, run on your tool.

  1. STEP 1

    FIND REAL RIVALS

    We find tools that actually do the same job as yours, and check that they can. A fake rival gives a fake result, so we throw those out.

  2. STEP 2

    SEND THE REQUESTS

    Hundreds of everyday requests go to AI models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, with your tool and the rivals all available.

  3. STEP 3

    SHOW WHO WON

    You get the grid: every request, which tool got it, and the AI's reason for skipping yours.

  4. STEP 4

    REWRITE AND RETEST

    We rewrite your descriptions and test them on requests they were never tuned for. If it didn't help, we tell you.

03 / WHAT ELSE WE CHECK

A tool's name
isn't proof.

Before we call something a rival, we check what it can really do.

6 OF 10

Claims vs. reality.

We looked at 10 public tools that all say they can search the web and read a page. Only 6 can read a page you hand them. Two need an extra step. Two we couldn't tell.

6 OF 6

We run the tools too.

We asked real tools from Microsoft, Twilio, AWS and Context7 six questions. All six gave a relevant answer.

1 TEST STOPPED

No fair rivals, no score.

For one tool we could only find two real rivals. Three is our minimum, so we stopped instead of making up a result.

Plainly: the test used the fast, low-cost models from each company (GPT-4.1 mini, Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash), the kind apps and agents commonly use to pick tools. It shows what the AI chooses in a controlled test, not your traffic. Your numbers will be your own.
