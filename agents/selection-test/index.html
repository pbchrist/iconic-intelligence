01 / HOW IT WORKS

Same job. Several tools.
Which one wins?

Getting listed means people can find your tool. This test shows whether the AI actually uses it.

  1. STEP 1

    FIND REAL RIVALS

    We find tools that genuinely do the same job as yours, and check that they really can. A fake rival makes a fake result, so we throw those out.

  2. STEP 2

    COUNT THE PICKS

    We give AI models dozens of everyday requests with all the tools available, and count how often each one gets picked.

  3. STEP 3

    FIND OUT WHY

    We show you who won your jobs and the reasons the AI gave for passing you over.

  4. STEP 4

    FIX IT AND RETEST

    We rewrite your tool descriptions and test again on requests the rewrite never saw. If it didn't help, we tell you.

02 / WHAT WE'VE FOUND

Real tests.
Real numbers.

From our own testing in November 2026, on public tools anyone can connect to.

PICKED 3 OUT OF 48

Good tool, bad description.

A working web-search tool lost almost every job to three rivals. The AI's reason, over and over: the description didn't make clear what the tool could do.

2 PICKS BECAME 6

Better words, more picks.

We rewrote its two descriptions and tested on 24 new requests. It went from 2 picks to 6, and didn't lose any job it had won before.

ONLY 6 OF 10

Names aren't proof.

We checked 10 public tools that all claim to search the web and read a page. Only 6 can read a page directly. Two need an extra step. Two were unclear.

We also test the tools themselves, not just their labels. We sent six real questions to tools from Microsoft, Twilio, AWS and Context7. All six gave a relevant answer. These were small tests, and we say so: they show what this measures, not a guarantee of what your results will be.
