Problems worth solving

Reconcile the numbers, not just the narrative.

Missing or extra records

Compare two snapshots using stable identifiers and separate observed disappearance from unproven explanations.

Duplicate identifiers

Flag repeated VINs, stock numbers, order IDs, or other keys that can inflate reported counts.

Changed values

Identify price or field changes and distinguish true changes from differences in reporting logic.

Conflicting totals

Build an evidence-first explanation of why dashboard, export, and search counts differ, including what still needs verification.

Illustrative example · Not customer data

Two inventory exports. Three different counts.

Suppose a dealership export reports 71 vehicles, a previous snapshot had 84, and a search page displays 79. A useful reconciliation checks duplicated identifiers, changed filters, missing rows, stale caches, and timing differences before calling any disappearance a sale.

Ask an agent: “Compare these two fictional inventory snapshots by VIN, flag duplicate keys and price changes, reconcile the totals, and separate observations from hypotheses.”

This is an example of the business question. The free tool supports bounded synthetic reconciliation, not a full production investigation of live dealership systems.
