The Context Tax: AI agents read ${ratio_round} times more than they write

Six months of production logs from three working AI agents. The answers they produce are under 1% of what they process. The rest is the agent re-reading its own conversation, and that re-reading grows faster than the work.

Of the reading, ${cached_m} tokens were context re-sent from earlier steps and served from the provider's cache. Only ${fresh_m} were new. Every step of an agent job sends the model the whole job so far: the instructions, the tools, every file it opened and every answer it got back. The model writes an average of ${out_per_step} tokens per step, then the next step sends all of it again, plus the new part. That re-reading is the context tax, and it is the largest line in these agents' workload.

02 · Method

Counted from the agents' own usage ledgers

The agents run on Hermes Agent, which records the provider's token counts after every model call. The counts land in each agent's local database as four counters per job and model: fresh input, cached input, cache writes and output. Hermes stores fresh input with cached tokens already subtracted. That makes the counters additive.

read(job)    = fresh_input + cached_input + cache_write
written(job) = output
steps(job)   = number of model calls in the job
growth       = slope of log(read) on log(steps), least squares across jobs

A job is one agent session: a scheduled report, a chat request, a hands-on working session or a sub-agent sent to do one piece of a larger task. A step is one call to the model. Single internal helper calls (title writing, memory lookups) are not jobs. The ${helpers} of them are excluded from every job figure. Including them moves the overall ratio to ${all_ratio} : 1.

Three scripts produce every number on this page: extract.py reads the databases read-only and writes jobs.csv (counts only, no message text). analyze.py computes results.json. build.py writes the figures into this page.

05 · Every step reads more

Each step costs more than the one before, until the ceiling

In a short job the model reads a median ${ps_short} tokens per step. In a 16-to-31-step job it reads ${ps_16}, ${ps_climb} times as much for every single call. Past 32 steps the figure holds flat at ${ps_32} and ${ps_64}, below the ${compress}-token compression limit configured for these agents.

The ratio of reading to writing climbs with it: ${rw_short} : 1 in short jobs, ${rw_64} : 1 in jobs of 64 steps or more, a ${rw_climb}-fold rise. A long job produces more, but it re-reads far more to do it.

07 · What it means for a firm

Budget for the steps, not the answers

Price agent work by how long it runs. The answer an agent hands back is ${written_pct}% of what it processed. A quote, budget or vendor comparison built on "how much will it write" misses the line that dominates.

Caching is a requirement, not an option. ${cached_pct}% of all reading here was context the provider had already seen and served from cache. A setup without caching processes every one of those tokens again as new input. Ask any AI vendor whether repeated context is cached, and at what rate it is charged.

Split long jobs. Jobs of 16+ steps were ${long_jobs_pct}% of the work and ${long_read_pct}% of the reading. Every step added to a long job re-sends everything before it, so the long tail is where the reading piles up.

Cap the context. These agents compress their working history at ${compress} tokens, and per-step reading levels off below it. An agent with no ceiling keeps climbing.

Read the meter before scaling. Hermes records these four counters for every job. One query on them shows where the reading goes, which jobs run long, and what a tenfold rollout does to the bill.

08 · Common questions

Frequently asked questions

What does an AI agent spend most of its tokens on?

Re-reading. Across ${jobs} production jobs the agents read ${ratio} tokens for every token they wrote, and ${cached_pct}% of the reading was context the agent had already seen earlier in the same job.

Why do long AI agent jobs cost so much more?

Every step re-sends the whole conversation so far. Jobs of 16 or more steps were ${long_jobs_pct}% of jobs and ${long_read_pct}% of all reading. Doubling a job's steps multiplied its reading by ${double} on average.

How do you keep AI agent costs under control?

Use a provider and setup that caches repeated context, split long jobs into short ones, cap the context with compression, and read the agent's own usage counters before scaling it.

Scope and disclosure

Limits

This is one operator's three agents, on one agent framework, over six months. The figures are tokens, not dollars: the agents ran on subscription plans and local hardware, so the ledgers carry no per-token charges, and this page converts none. The growth exponent compares different jobs, not the same job run at different lengths. Jobs that run long also tend to be different kinds of work. The per-step plateau sits below the configured compression limit; this study does not isolate compression as its cause.
