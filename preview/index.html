Client work · An auto dealership · Running now

The owner figured a rival was $500 to $800 cheaper. Truck for truck, the gap was $4,472.

It started with one question from the owners: why are rivals selling more pickups than we are? The owner's hunch was that one rival was $500 to $800 cheaper. So the AI read both lots and paired every truck with its exact twin: same year, same trim, same engine, same drive. The rival was cheaper on 20 of the 22 pairs, by $4,472 a truck on average. That is the price gap a shopper sees online, not lost revenue. It was more than five times what the owner thought.

Now it runs every day. Every morning it reads every pickup on the owner's lot and his rivals' lots, and three times a week it writes him a brief. He can text it a question from his phone any time. He never has to take its word for anything: every price in a brief links to the rival's own web page, so he can tap it and see it for himself before he acts.

The owner's verdict“Exceptional.”

What it did for him

  • It stopped a price war before it started.When every dealer repriced on the same day, it flagged a likely factory program, not a rival coming after them.
  • It found the real gap.The owner thought a rival was undercutting him by $800 a truck. The real number was $4,472.
  • It found what sells.Trucks priced $3,000 under market left the lot three times faster.
  • It caught the store's own mistake.It spotted a wrong price on the dealership's own website that staff had missed.
  • It grew from one question.It started as one question about one rival's prices. Now it watches its competitors across the whole lineup and hands the owner a brief three times a week: who moved, what expired, and the one call to make.
