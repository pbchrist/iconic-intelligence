The evidence.

Is today's AI being sold for less than it costs? Here is every source I rely on, rated by how solid it is, with the evidence against the claim included.

Compiled 2026-11-13. Every link below was fetched on that date and the quoted figure was read on the page. Quotes are verbatim fragments.

Ratings used in the tables:

  • STRONG: a primary statement (the company itself, its CEO, or its SEC filing) or a financial figure reported by a major outlet from company documents.
  • LIST-PRICE: a number computed from API list prices. It measures what the usage would cost a customer at retail, not what the lab spends to serve it.
  • SECONDARY: commentary, an analyst model, or a report of a report.

1. Read this first: what the evidence does not support

  1. No source states what a lab actually spends to serve a $200 subscriber. The "$200 plan costs them $2,000" line cannot be cited as fact. The closest text is Tom's Guide calling "$2,000 of usage" on a $200 plan a "long-standing rule of thumb", which is usage at list price, not cost. The $8,000 and $14,000 figures are also list price, and Tom's Guide says so: "these are API-equivalent figures, not what the companies actually spend."
  2. Anthropic's June 15, 2026 move of automated work off flat plans did not take effect. It was announced in May and paused on the day. The Decoder, 16 June 2026: "Anthropic has paused the billing overhaul it had announced for June 15, 2026." Do not cite it as a squeeze that happened. It can be cited as a squeeze that was announced and withdrawn.
  3. Anthropic reports an adjusted profit. Its amended S-1 (2 November 2026) shows adjusted operating income for Q2 and Q3 2026, and the FT reports "$1.70 in revenue for every $1 spent on computing power" in Q3. A skeptic will lead with this.
  4. OpenAI's margin on paying users is reported at about 70%. That is the share of revenue left after server costs for paying users, per The Information.
  5. No source found gives a date for a price jump or predicts a vendor collapse.

What the evidence does support: the labs lose money overall, the heaviest subscription users get far more than they pay for at list price, three vendors have said in their own words that flat pricing was not covering cost, and the labs carry compute commitments in the hundreds of billions that must be paid regardless of usage.

5. Price versus cost studies

STRONG

Peer-level academic study (MIT authors). It cuts both ways. Price for a fixed level of benchmark performance falls "around 5x to 10x per year". But "the price of running frontier models is rising between 3x to 18x per year". The second half supports the thesis: staying at the frontier keeps getting more expensive.

arXiv 2511.23455, Gundlach, Lynch, Mertens, Thompson, submitted 28 November 2025, revised 23 March 2026 · arxiv.org ↗
SECONDARY

"Frontier models are getting more expensive, not cheaper." "Per-token costs are falling. But total costs per task are rising." Anthropic's gross margin was "negative 94% last year" (2024), expected to reach 50% in 2025; no source cited for the Anthropic figures.

SaaStr, Jason Lemkin, 24 December 2025 · saastr.com ↗
SECONDARY

Open-model price comparison: DeepSeek-V4-Pro "usually costs around 1.74 per 1 million input tokens and $3.48 per 1 million output tokens with neutral vendors", against leading labs at "$2-5 per million input tokens, to $12-25 per million output tokens." The author uses this to argue API prices are above cost (see section 6).

LessWrong, Yair Halberstadt, 11 May 2026 · lesswrong.com ↗

6. Counter-evidence a skeptic will cite

Strong

Anthropic is adjusted-profitable and compute is covered by revenue. It "achieved adjusted profitability for a second consecutive quarter" and earned "$1.70 in revenue for every $1 spent on computing power during the third quarter". The article's own caveat: "adjusted profitability and positive cash flow do not necessarily establish profitability under standard accounting measures".

Yahoo Finance (reporting the Financial Times), 15 October 2026 · finance.yahoo.com ↗
Strong

Q3 2026 adjusted operating profit of $2.463 billion at a 15% margin. "the adjusted result excludes stock-based pay and a large non-cash donation-matching charge." The GAAP result for the same quarter was the $0.567 billion operating loss in section 3.

INDmoney, 3 November 2026 · indmoney.com ↗
Strong

OpenAI makes money on the compute for paying users. Compute margin "jumped from around 35 percent in January 2024 to roughly 70 percent by October 2025." Defined as "the share of revenue left after paying for server costs from paying users." It excludes free users and training. "Anthropic is expected to reach 53 percent by year's end."

The Decoder (reporting The Information), 22 December 2025 · the-decoder.com ↗
Strong

Same report: "As of October, that margin came to 70%", "up from 52% at the end of 2024".

PYMNTS, 21 December 2025 · pymnts.com ↗
Strong

Inference keeps getting cheaper. "For an LLM of equivalent performance, the cost is decreasing by 10x every year." "The cost of LLM inference has dropped by a factor of 1,000 in 3 years." Author's caveat: "its rate may slow down."

a16z, Guido Appenzeller, 12 November 2024 · a16z.com ↗
Strong

"the price to achieve GPT-4's performance on a set of PhD-level science questions fell by 40x per year." Range "from 9x to 900x per year."

Epoch AI, 12 March 2025 · epoch.ai ↗
Medium

Independent estimate that API prices are far above raw compute cost: "$3/15 per million tokens vs ~$0.01/3 actual costs", "80-95%+ gross margins", and Claude Max plans priced at a markup over cost. The author: "This is really napkin math. I don't have any experience at running frontier models at scale." Uses an open model as a proxy and ignores training.

Martin Alderson, 27 August 2025 · martinalderson.com ↗
Medium

"the marginal cost to respond to an AI API call is significantly lower than the price of that call." Covers serving cost only, not training or infrastructure.

LessWrong, Yair Halberstadt, 11 May 2026 · lesswrong.com ↗
Medium

Axios's own bottom line: "The costs of AI will keep going down." Token pricing "has fallen, partly due to a massive efficiency jump in inference."

Axios, 12 March 2026 (date from URL) · axios.com ↗
Medium

Anthropic backed off a price squeeze under competitive pressure (section 4), and Gigazine reports, citing the WSJ, that OpenAI is considering lowering token prices. Competition is currently pushing prices down, not up.

The Decoder, 16 June 2026; Gigazine, 12 June 2026 · links in sections 2 and 4

7. The position that survives a skeptic

  • API inference sold at list price to paying customers appears to be profitable at the compute level. Saying "all inference is sold below cost" will lose the argument.
  • The subsidy that is documented sits in three places: flat-rate plans for heavy users (Altman, Anthropic, GitHub and Cursor have each said so in their own words), free users, and the training and infrastructure bill that revenue does not yet cover (OpenAI's $25B forecast burn for 2026, Anthropic's GAAP operating loss in Q3 2026).
  • The pressure that could force prices up is the fixed commitments: $895.4 billion at Anthropic per its S-1/A, about 80% of the earlier $518 billion non-cancellable, and $665 billion of planned model spend at OpenAI through 2030.
  • The evidence that the frontier itself is getting more expensive to use, even while old capability gets cheaper, is the arXiv study: frontier running price "rising between 3x to 18x per year".
